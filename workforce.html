Step by step

What it looks like on screen.

Real screens from a BPMSquare workspace. Company and employee names are fictional.

1

Rates are set once.

Three rungs, most specific wins: a rate on the project itself, else the person's employment type, else the workspace default. Cost rates stay internal and show margin on the preview only.

2

Projects and sub-projects, numbered inside the parent.

A project needs only a name; its ID is assigned. Parts sit under it as Level 1, 2 and 3 (PRJ-0005.1, PRJ-0005.1.2), each with its own people, shifts, sites and budget. Hours on a part roll up into the project.

3

Tell the project who works on it.

Four rungs are checked at every check-in, top to bottom: the roster for that date, linked people, linked shifts, linked sites. If two projects match at once the hours are left unassigned and shown in amber — never guessed, never hidden, never billed.

4

Put people on a project for specific dates.

From the roster, pick a project, the dates and the people. Their shift stays; only where their hours go changes. This beats every other rung, so it is how a supervisor settles who is on what.

5

Employees just punch.

Nothing changes on the phone or the kiosk. The project is resolved on the server as the check-in is recorded and shown on the punch screen, so the person can see where their day is going.

6

Hours arrive on the project.

This month, the last 90 days or the last 12 months, with headcount, percentage of budget and every part beneath. The same figure appears on the customer's account page and in analytics, because it is one calculation.

Billing

The invoice is raised from the hours, not typed from them.

Pick a period, choose one line for the project or one per sub-project, and see exactly what the invoice will say before anything is created.

7

Preview, with the rate that applied and why.

Actual minutes, never rounded to a block. Tax as it will print. Cost and margin for admins only. Anything that would make the invoice wrong is named in words: no account linked, no rate, a period already invoiced, unassigned hours in the window.

  • No double billing: an overlapping period is refused, on the project, a part of it, or a parent that covered it
  • Hours added after invoicing are offered as a top-up, never a re-bill
  • Month-end drafts can run on their own for every account-linked project
8

A draft invoice, one line per sub-project.

Quantity in hours, the rate, and the period on every line. It lands in Invoices as a draft you can still edit, with payments, PDF and history like any other invoice.

9

Email it, PDF attached.

Tick the account's contacts, add any address by hand, edit the message and send. The draft becomes Sent on the first successful send and every recipient is logged. Demo and test workspaces are locked to an internal inbox, so no test document can ever reach a real customer.

10

The customer's page knows too.

Every project for the account, hours this month, and how far each has been invoiced. Raise the next project from here, already linked.

Rules the system enforces

So nobody has to remember them.

Actual minutes. Line quantity is hours to two decimals; amount is quantity × rate, so the printed line agrees with itself.
A session belongs to the day it started, in the workspace timezone. Night shifts count on the day they began.
Rate ladder: project, then nearest parent, then employment type, then default. Zero means unset, never free.
Stamped at punch time. Changing a link or the roster affects future punches only; a billed period cannot change underneath its invoice.
No double billing. Periods covered by a non-cancelled invoice are refused. Deleting a draft or cancelling an invoice frees the period.
Unassigned hours are never billed. They are a warning to settle on the roster.
Cost never leaves the workspace. Margin shows to admins on the preview and nowhere else — not on the invoice, not on the API.
Everything is on the API. Projects, hours per period, invoices from hours, and one row per work session with its rate — plus MCP for AI clients.
